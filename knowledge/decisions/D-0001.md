# D-0001 — Diving

nfx sent a brief for two crewed submarines (2026-10-07; `knowledge/sources/`). The Explorer has
4 seats, a 9×3 hold, twin floods, 4 upgrade and 2 weapon slots, and is slow and tough. The Scout
has 1 seat, a 9×1 locker, a single spotlight, 2 upgrade and 1 weapon slot, and is fast and
fragile.

The brief recommended an Immersive Aircraft addon. Its reason was that it read Vanilla Wheels as
a jar with no source, needing ASM patches. Rusty, 2026-10-07: "it's probably an extension of
vanilla wheels. It's a new vehicle." The approved plan is
`~/.claude/plans/peppy-scribbling-lollipop.md`.

**Decision**:

- **Layering.** A submarine is a Vanilla Wheels vehicle: its look, seats, chests, fuel, paint,
  lights, keys, condition, repair and the lift are that protocol's.
  - Its id is also in this protocol's synced registry `submersibles:submarine`
    (`data/<ns>/submersibles/submarine/<name>.json`), which says how it dives.
  - `Submarine` extends `Vehicle` and is made for those ids through Vanilla Wheels'
    `api/VehicleKinds` (its D-0030).
  - Its hull of boxes, its crash judging and its up/down/get-out keys are Vanilla Wheels'
    (its D-0031, moved there from Rotorcraft on Rusty's call for this protocol).
  - Submarine mods ship data only.
- **The model** (`domain/Dive`, pure) is stepped where Vanilla Wheels' boat rule says: on the
  pilot's client, re-run by the server, and on the server alone when nobody pilots it.
  - **Medium.** How much of the hull is under water, 0 to 1: in each of its hull's columns (each
    box's corners and middle), the share of its height under the water's top, averaged. It is
    continuous, so the hull floats steadily; probe points in rows would rock it a row at a time.
  - **In water:**
    - the propeller drives it along its heading at `engineSpeed` times its power;
    - it loses `friction` plus the hull's own drag of its speed a tick, and settles at
      `thrust * (1 - drag) / drag`;
    - it goes astern at a third;
    - it keeps a fifth of any slide a tick, so the velocity turns with the body;
    - the rudder turns it at `yawSpeed`, a third as fast at rest.
  - **Depth.** Submerged and powered, Left Shift dives and Space climbs at `verticalSpeed`,
    eased by a tenth of it a tick.
    - Let go, it holds its depth. It is trimmed to neutral, so holding depth burns no fuel.
    - With no fuel, or as a wreck, it is buoyant and rises at `rise`.
    - At the surface it floats with `draft` of its height under water. Space cannot lift it out;
      Left Shift takes it down.
  - **Out of the water** it falls (the game's gravity) and makes no way: beached. Pack it and set
    it down in water.
  - **Currents** push it by `wind`, the share of the game's push on a swimmer.
  - **Its pose:** nose down diving under way, nose up climbing, level sinking at rest, banked into
    a turn, eased by `stabilizer`. It is drawn through Vanilla Wheels' pose, and beached it rides
    the ground as a car does.
- **Fuel.** It burns while the pilot holds the throttle, the rudder or the planes (the controls
  reach the server on a change). Parked or holding depth it burns none. A rate from its upgrades
  scales the burn (Vanilla Wheels' `fuelRate`, D-0002 here).
- **Nobody aboard is hurt.**
  - The crew breathe aboard: every passenger's air is topped up every tick (`air`, true by
    default).
  - No fall reaches a rider.
  - A wreck under water is not destroyed. Unpowered, it rises with its crew aboard and becomes a
    packed wreck at the surface.
  - It runs nothing over and breaks nothing fragile.
- **Crashes wear it**, by Vanilla Wheels' `Crash` at the profile's speeds (by default safe 0.15,
  the same for the seabed, a wreck at 0.8), divided by its `durability`. They are judged on the
  server from the moves it steps or the pilot's client reports, once an impact. Every other wear
  (a mob, an explosion, fire) is divided by the durability too; a player's own blow is still a
  knock toward packing it.
- **Getting out:** with the get-out key (R), at the hatch the profile names when there is room
  there (in the water or the air), else a little below it, else Vanilla Wheels' rule. Under water
  the rider comes out swimming with full air.
- **Set down on water:** a packed submarine aimed at open water within reach floats there at its
  draft, through Vanilla Wheels' own placement (its D-0031). Aimed at a block, it is set on the
  block's face, as any vehicle; one set on the seabed rises.
- **No crush depth or pressure damage.** Minecraft's oceans give it nothing to do.

**Why**:
- A submarine shares everything a Vanilla Wheels vehicle has but its motion.
- Trimmed to neutral, a submarine you swim out of is still there when you swim back.
- Buoyant when dead, one that ran out of fuel at depth stays recoverable.
- Breathing aboard is the role of a submarine; one that drowned its crew would be broken.

**Alternatives rejected**:
- *An Immersive Aircraft addon* (the brief's): Rusty's call, above.
- *Burning fuel to hold depth*: a parked submarine would empty its tank and surface while its
  crew explored a wreck.
- *Probe points for the medium*: the float would rock a row at a time.
- *A crush depth*: no gameplay at Minecraft's depths.

**Verification**:
- JUnit `DiveTest` (13): the medium × power × input partitions; top speed; spool; turning in
  place; dive and climb eased; the surface; buoyancy; beached; currents with and without wind;
  the pose; a wall; submersion's columns; bounds refused.
- `DiveGameTests` (9), the box submarine in a tank:
  - it holds its depth with nothing held, and parked;
  - without fuel it rises;
  - Space brings it up to float at its draft (±0.08) and Shift takes it down;
  - beached it makes no way;
  - an underwater wall stops it and wears it once, half with a reinforcement;
  - a villager aboard keeps full air for 400 ticks while one overboard drowns;
  - Shift keeps the pilot aboard and they get out at the hatch;
  - a wreck at depth comes up with its rider unhurt and is packed at the surface;
  - a packed submarine aimed at water is set down floating at its draft.
- Each rule was run against its mutation (`knowledge/PROJECT.md`, Gate).
